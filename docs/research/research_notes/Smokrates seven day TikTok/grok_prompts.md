# Prompting Grok Imagine for a 5–15 second 9:16 insert

Fetched 14 October 2026 from docs.x.ai (pages brand themselves SpaceXSI) and the x.ai news posts linked below. This note is the video and TikTok-insert layer. It does not replace the stills taxonomy in `Prompting Grok Imagine for Reliable Image Results.md`. There is no TikTok-specific prompt mode in these docs.

The recurring man is fictional. Do not put the artist name, or the word SMOKRATES, in a model prompt. Lock him with an approved reference image after the first still. Hex values below are the studio’s code colors. The image guide asks for color words, not hex, so the paste-ready prompts use the words.

## What does the current image prompting guide require?

### Takeaway
For `grok-imagine-image-2.0`, xAI wants one photograph in prose, subject first, four to six sentences, with aspect ratio, resolution, and quality set on the request. Generate when the subject is wrong. Edit when the subject and layout are already right and only one to three details change. Multi-image edit accepts up to five sources. The guide does not document a video prompt, a text-rendering mode, or a character-ID token.

### Cited Findings
- The image prompting guide, sitemap `lastmod` 2026-10-08, covers `grok-imagine-image-2.0` stills: a new image from text, or an edit of an existing one. “The score that matters is whether the subject in the picture matches the subject in the prompt. Palette, film grain, and styling come after that.” — [Image Prompting Guide](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/images/prompting-guide); [sitemap](https://docs.x.ai/sitemap.xml)
- Request fields shown for generation are `model`, `prompt`, `aspect_ratio`, `resolution`, `quality`, `response_format`, and `n`. Edits use `POST /v1/images/edits` with an `image` object. “Lock `aspect_ratio` across a series.” `quality` accepts `low`, `medium`, or `auto`. “`auto` currently serves `low` on generation and `medium` on edit. Pin `medium` and `2k` when fine detail has to be recognizable.” — [Image Prompting Guide](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/images/prompting-guide)
- Order, kept the same across a series: (1) subject, including form, what supports it, and the setting; (2) details and focal point, materials, count, and camera angle, written once in the subject paragraph and repeated in the same words in composition; (3) art direction, color fields and two or three named props; (4) light, source, direction, hardness, and an exposure bright enough to read the subject; (5) subject colors and background colors as separate fields; (6) composition, crop, what the frame cuts off, what stays sharp, one scene. — [Image Prompting Guide](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/images/prompting-guide)
- “Write prose that describes one photograph.” A line that reads like an instruction describes an attribute of that single picture, because a generation has no before-and-after. “A prompt that leads with mood, film stock, or a list of things to avoid produces a styled scene whose subject is a stand-in.” Describe materials physically. “A long avoid list is easy to ignore, and it spends the prompt on objects that then show up anyway.” State a count, and pair a constraint with the thing you want. Say texture once. “Slightly underexposed” and “crushed shadows” pull the picture dark. Leave out chromatic fringing, dust, and scratches. — [Image Prompting Guide](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/images/prompting-guide)
- “A single still outside a series needs four to six sentences: subject and details, then setting, then light, then crop.” The three published examples are that shape. They do not put the ratio string inside the prose. The official portrait example is a woman in her sixties, waist-up, 35mm, eye level, face sharp. — [Image Prompting Guide](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/images/prompting-guide)
- Generate when there is no picture, or when the subject is a different object, a key element is missing, or the count is off. Edit when subject and layout are right and one to three details must change. “Lead with those changes. Follow with one sentence that keeps the same subject, angle, crop, and prop placement.” Official edit sentence: “Replace the metal watch strap with a brown leather strap with cream stitching. Keep the same wrist, the same shirt cuff, the same angle, and the same desk.” “An edit that opens with ‘keep everything’ barely moves. An edit that pastes in a full new prompt restages the scene.” If a fix misses, change the words and point at the part. Do not repeat “more realistic.” Judge the picture against the original prompt. On the next pass, change only the detail that failed. — [Image Prompting Guide](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/images/prompting-guide)
- Image `aspect_ratio` values include `9:16` for “Widescreen, mobile, stories,” plus `1:1`, `16:9`, `4:3`, `3:4`, `3:2`, `2:3`, `2:1`, `1:2`, `19.5:9`, `9:19.5`, `20:9`, `9:20`, `21:9`, `5:2`, and `auto`. Omitted ratio defaults to `auto`. Resolution is `1k` (default) or `2k`. The generation page does not state pixel width and height for `9:16` or for `2k`. `n` is 1–10. — [Image Generation](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/images/generation)
- Editing is natural language over a public URL, a base64 data URI, or a Files API `file_id`. The OpenAI SDK `images.edit()` multipart call is unsupported. Multi-turn editing chains each output into the next input. The editing page points prompt wording back at the prompting guide. — [Image Editing](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/images/editing)
- Multi-image editing uses up to five source images in one edit. “By default, the output aspect ratio follows the first input image.” Override with `aspect_ratio`. Kinds of source can be mixed. The page does not document `<IMAGE_0>` tokens, role labels, or a prompt template. — [Multi-Image Editing](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/images/multi-image-editing); [Imagine API](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/imagine)
- Current image model slug is `grok-imagine-image-2.0`, text and image to image, $0.04 per image, 6 requests per second, regions us-east-1 and us-west-2. Release notes: on 2 November 2026, `grok-imagine-image-quality` is retired onto `grok-imagine-image-2.0` at `quality: low`. `grok-imagine-image` (1.0) is not affected. August notes: `quality` default moved from `medium` to `auto`, and image edit sources rose from 3 to 5. — [grok-imagine-image-2.0](https://docs.x.ai/developers/models/grok-imagine-image-2.0); [Models](https://docs.x.ai/developers/models); [Release Notes](https://docs.x.ai/developers/release-notes)

### Inferences
- For a 9:16 poster still, set `aspect_ratio` to `9:16`, `resolution` to `2k`, and `quality` to `medium` on the request. Do not rely on the word “9:16” inside the prose, and do not leave ratio on `auto`.
- A still that will be animated should already be the opening frame: one person, one pose, feet and hands inside the frame if they will move, face readable, and a flat cream field reserved for type. That composition follows the guide’s “one scene” and “what stays sharp” rules. The guide never says this is an animation requirement.
- Pose plus place is two changes, inside the “one to three details” edit rule, only if the face and layout are already right. If the edit restages him, the guide says to narrow the words, not to paste a full new prompt.

### Gaps
- No pixel dimensions for a `2k` `9:16` still were found. “1080×1920” is not a documented image size.
- No official image-edit syntax for “this face from image A, this place from image B” was found. The `<IMAGE_n>` pattern is not on the multi-image page.
- The prompting guide does not mention on-image text, posters, or brand lettering.

## What is the current video model, and what prompt rules exist?

### Takeaway
There is no video prompting guide. `https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/prompting-guide.md` returned 404 on 14 October 2026, and `llms.txt` lists no video prompting page. The current generation model is `grok-imagine-video-1.5`. Official motion language is natural prose for the shot: what happens next, plus camera, light, and sound. Duration, ratio, and resolution are request fields. A pinned loop or a start-and-end pair is reference-to-video and stops at 720p. Native 1080p is text-to-video and image-to-video only.

### Cited Findings
- `llms.txt` lists image prompting, video generation, overview, image-to-video, reference-to-video, video editing, and video extension. It does not list a video prompting guide. — [llms.txt](https://docs.x.ai/llms.txt)
- Model page: `grok-imagine-video-1.5`, modalities text and image to video, aliases `grok-imagine-video-1.5-preview` and `grok-imagine-video-1.5-2026-05-30`, $0.080 per second, 10 requests per second. The models index also lists `grok-imagine-video` at $0.050 per second and `grok-imagine-video-1.5-lite` at $0.020 per second. — [grok-imagine-video-1.5](https://docs.x.ai/developers/models/grok-imagine-video-1.5); [Models](https://docs.x.ai/developers/models)
- Overview, sitemap `lastmod` 2026-10-08: use `grok-imagine-video-1.5-lite` for simple videos and `grok-imagine-video-1.5` when you need references, voices, pinned frames, or native 1080p. Clips are 1–15 seconds on all three models. — [Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview)
- Capability table on that overview: `grok-imagine-video-1.5` does text-to-video and image-to-video at native 1080p, up to 14 reference images at 15 seconds and 720p, up to 3 voice references, first and last frame, and up to 4 mid-video keyframes. It does not do video editing or extension. `grok-imagine-video-1.5-lite` does text-to-video and image-to-video with lip-synced speech, 1080p by upscaling 720p, and does not support reference images, voices, first and last frame, or keyframes. `grok-imagine-video` does text-to-video and image-to-video up to 720p, up to 7 reference images at 10 seconds and 720p, no voices and no pinned frames, editing of a source up to 8.7 seconds, and extension that adds 2–10 seconds. — [Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview)
- Aspect ratios on all three video models: `1:1`, `16:9`, `9:16`, `4:3`, `3:4`, `3:2`, `2:3`, `21:9`, `5:2`. Default is `16:9`. Resolution choices are `480p` (documented as the default), `720p`, and `1080p`, with the model caps above. “Image-to-video output always matches the input image’s aspect ratio; `aspect_ratio` is ignored.” — [Video Generation](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation)
- `duration` is 1–15 seconds, default 8. Reference-to-video on `grok-imagine-video` allows up to 10 seconds. Editing cannot set `duration`, `aspect_ratio`, or `resolution`. The edited clip keeps the source duration, ratio, and resolution, capped at 720p, and the source itself is capped at 8.7 seconds. — [Video Generation](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation); [Video Editing](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/editing)
- Extension is `grok-imagine-video` only. Source 2–15 seconds. `duration` is the added portion, 2–10 seconds, default 6, not the total. Output keeps the source ratio and resolution, capped at 720p. The prompt describes what happens next. An official extension example is “The shot pans to an over the shoulder perspective. Calm controlled scene.” — [Video Extension](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/extension)
- Text-to-video: “Describe the shot, including camera movement, lighting, and sound.” On `grok-imagine-video-1.5` and `grok-imagine-video-1.5-lite`, “the model renders a first frame from the prompt, then animates it.” July release notes say the same text-to-image-then-image-to-video path for 1.5. The generation markdown note had the two model names stripped in the fetch (“On  and ,”), so the overview and the release notes are the named sources. — [Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview); [Release Notes](https://docs.x.ai/developers/release-notes)
- Image-to-video: sending `image` alone is image-to-video. “The still you pass as `image` becomes the first frame, and the prompt describes what happens next.” The prompt is optional. If omitted, “the model generates a video from the image alone.” Image input is a public URL, a base64 data URI, or a `file_id`. Combining `image` with `last_frame`, `keyframes`, or `reference_images` is reference-to-video, with that image as the pinned first frame. Official image-to-video wording in the Imagine overview is “Make the water crash down and slowly pan out the camera,” `duration` 12. — [Image-to-Video](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/image-to-video); [Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview); [Imagine API](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/imagine); [Videos REST](https://docs.x.ai/developers/rest-api-reference/inference/videos)
- Generated video includes an audio track by default, with lip-synced speech on 1.5 and 1.5-lite. `generate_audio: false` requests a silent video. The published silent example is “A paper boat drifting down a rain-soaked street.” — [Video Generation](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation)
- Voices exist only as reference-to-video on `grok-imagine-video-1.5`: up to 3 preset `voice_id` values from the text-to-speech roster, tagged `<AUDIO_0>` onward. An unknown id returns 400 with the list. The capability page says caller-supplied voice audio is for trusted partners on request. The REST field list also documents a `reference_audios.url` of at most 15 seconds, mutually exclusive with `voice_id`. Those two pages disagree. Example ids on the pages are `eve`, `leo`, and `ara`. — [Reference-to-Video](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/reference-to-video); [Videos REST](https://docs.x.ai/developers/rest-api-reference/inference/videos)
- Reference images do not lock the first frame, unlike image-to-video. Refer to them as `<IMAGE_0>`, `<IMAGE_1>`, and so on. If `image` is also set, the pinned first frame takes `<IMAGE_0>` and the references start at `<IMAGE_1>`. “With many references, give each one a single role and a fixed place in the scene, keep the cast small, and keep props static.” Limits on the 2026-10-09 reference page: 14 images on 1.5, 7 on `grok-imagine-video`, up to 15 seconds and 720p on 1.5. The July news post said “up to seven references.” The October page is the later figure for 1.5. — [Reference-to-Video](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/reference-to-video); [Imagine Video 1.5 with References](https://x.ai/news/grok-imagine-video-1-5-references)
- The REST usage object records prompt text tokens “consumed by the prompt-rewriting (upsampler) LLM” and rewritten-prompt text tokens plus reasoning tokens on the way out. No maximum prompt length is stated. A failed job can return `invalid_argument` for “a prompt that is too long,” along with an unsupported duration, a bad image, conflicting modes, or moderation. The sample message for an empty prompt is “Prompt cannot be empty.” — [Videos REST](https://docs.x.ai/developers/rest-api-reference/inference/videos); [Video Generation](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation)
- The capability page says `prompt` is optional for any request that includes `image`, `reference_images`, `last_frame`, or `keyframes`, and required only for text-to-video. The REST reference says `prompt` is required for text-to-video and reference-to-video, and optional for image-to-video. — [Video Generation](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation); [Videos REST](https://docs.x.ai/developers/rest-api-reference/inference/videos)
- The REST field list fetched on 14 October 2026 includes `aspect_ratio`, `duration`, `image`, `keyframes`, `reference_images`, `reference_audios`, `resolution`, and `prompt`. It does not list `last_frame` or `generate_audio`. Both of those fields are in working request bodies on the capability pages. The Vercel AI SDK does not expose `last_frame` or `keyframes`. Send those on the REST body. — [Videos REST](https://docs.x.ai/developers/rest-api-reference/inference/videos); [Reference-to-Video](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/reference-to-video); [Video Generation](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation)
- News, not a prompt manual. 3 June 2026 preview: give a starting frame and a prompt for the motion. “Direct the shot with natural-language prompts. Describe the camera move, the pacing, and the sound design.” The model “holds detail and lighting from the input frame.” Preview cap named in that post was 720p. The code sample prompt is “Slow cinematic push-in as embers drift across the battlefield and the helmet’s crest stirs in the wind.” 16 June 2026: 1.5 generally available. “Give it a starting image, describe the motion, and choose your resolution and duration.” Same prompt sample. - No official page found lists these as current failure modes: garbled on-screen words, charts, captions, lower-thirds, on-screen counts, identity drift, or a camera verb that fails to move the subject. Official image rules that do transfer in spirit are the avoid-list warning, the one-scene rule, and the ban on restaging with a full new prompt. Official video samples that show the intended shape are single-motion lines: a swishing tail, a drifting boat, one dolly, one push-in, one pan out. — [Image Prompting Guide](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/images/prompting-guide); [Video Generation](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation); [Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview)

### Inferences
- A 6-second 9:16 insert must set `duration` to 6. The default is 8. Text-to-video must also set `aspect_ratio` to `9:16`, or the default `16:9` wins. Image-to-video ignores `aspect_ratio` and copies the still, so the still has to be 9:16 already. Set `resolution` to `1080p` on a plain image-to-video. The documented default is `480p`.
- Do not describe the music bed in the video prompt if the clip will be cut to the existing dub. The docs generate audio unless `generate_audio` is false, and the overview tells you to describe sound. Silence is the request flag, not a sentence in the prompt.
- Because an upsampler rewrites the prompt, a short motion sentence is safer than a style paragraph. The docs do not say the upsampler ignores any particular words.
- Native 1080p and a pinned end frame are not available on the same request. `image` plus `last_frame` is reference-to-video, and reference-to-video on 1.5 is up to 720p. Lite can upscale image-to-video to 1080p but cannot pin frames. For a 1080×1920 master, either accept a 720p pinned clip and scale it in the edit, or stay on image-to-video at `1080p` and do not pin the end.

### Gaps
- No numeric prompt-length cap was found. “Too long” is only an error example.
- No official sentence count for video prompts was found. The four-to-six-sentence rule is the image guide.
- No official statement was found that camera verbs fail to move the subject, that multi-action prompts collapse, or that identity drifts inside one image-to-video clip.
- Exact pixel size of 9:16 at `1080p` or `720p` was not stated. The docs say the labels, not 1080×1920 or 720×1280.
- The REST page and the capability pages disagree on whether a reference-to-video prompt is required, and on whether caller-supplied voice audio is a public field or a partner feature. `last_frame` and `generate_audio` are in capability examples and absent from the REST field list that was fetched.

## Loop, start-to-end travel, and one camera move

### Takeaway
Official control is frame pins on `grok-imagine-video-1.5`, not magic words. The same image as `image` and `last_frame` is the documented loop. Two different stills from one edited scene are the documented start and end, and one camera or motion sentence steers the middle. A single camera move on a locked opening is image-to-video: the still is frame one, and the prompt says what happens next. All pinned modes are 720p.

### Cited Findings
- “Pass the same image as `image` and `last_frame` to make a loop.” Repeated on the reference page: “To loop a clip, pass the same image as `image` and `last_frame`.” — [Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview); [Reference-to-Video](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/reference-to-video)
- `last_frame` “pins the exact last frame of the clip. The video ends arriving on that image rather than re-rendering it as a reference.” `image` plus `last_frame` means the model “interpolates between the two.” `last_frame` alone generates an opening and lands on the pin. `prompt` is optional. “Include one to steer motion and camera work between the frames; omit it to let the frames alone drive the clip.” The published prompt is “The camera dollies from the sunlit doorway to the window, settling on the closing frame,” with `duration` 8, `aspect_ratio` `16:9`, and `resolution` `720p`. — [Reference-to-Video](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/reference-to-video)
- “Pins work best when they share a scene: generate one still, then make the others with image editing so only what should change differs.” Keyframes pin up to four images strictly inside the clip, greater than 0 and less than `duration`, on a 1/3-second grid, at least 1/3 second apart. Endpoints stay on `image` and `last_frame`. The page’s keyframe heading is “a year that loops,” but the fetched markdown does not include a separate loop prompt under that heading. The loop instruction is the same-image rule above. — [Reference-to-Video](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/reference-to-video)
- Image plus `last_frame`, `keyframes`, `reference_images`, or `reference_audios` is reference-to-video. Reference-to-video on 1.5 is up to 720p, not native 1080p. — [Video Generation](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation); [Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview)
- A camera move without an end pin is the image-to-video pattern. June 2026 news: one natural-language sentence for camera, pacing, and sound, from a starting image. The docs’ own verbs are dolly, push-in, and pan out, each as the motion of the shot. — [Grok Imagine 1.5 Preview](https://x.ai/news/grok-imagine-1-5); [Imagine API](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/imagine)
- Anecdotal, May 2026, tested by the author of `imagine-gallery` on `grok-imagine-image`, `grok-imagine-image-pro`, and `grok-imagine-video`, not on the October 2026 1.5 pin API. That note says one primary action per second, sequential beats rather than simultaneous actions, and one camera move per shot. It treats “slow push in with a pan left” as too many moves. It also says a 15-second clip “occasionally lose[s] coherence in the middle.” Its prices and the claim that `image` plus `reference_images` is undefined contradict the October docs, which price 1.5 at $0.080 per second and explicitly allow that combination. Use it only where it extends the docs. — [prompting-tips.md](https://github.com/Kearney-Evan-MI/imagine-gallery/blob/0e3bc99a715ba90a800bbbf34ee2382edbfdf095/integrations/grok-imagine-toolkit/references/prompting-tips.md); [known-quirks.md](https://github.com/Kearney-Evan-MI/imagine-gallery/blob/0e3bc99a715ba90a800bbbf34ee2382edbfdf095/integrations/grok-imagine-toolkit/references/known-quirks.md)

### Inferences
- Seamless loop for this insert: approve one 9:16 still, send it as both `image` and `last_frame` on `grok-imagine-video-1.5`, `duration` 6, `resolution` `720p`, `generate_audio` false. The prompt should describe one motion that leaves and comes back, so the interpolated middle agrees with identical end frames. The pin is what makes the seam. The prompt does not.
- Start-to-end travel: generate the start still, edit a second still in the same scene so only position or pose changes, send them as `image` and `last_frame`, and write one sentence of travel that names both ends. Do not invent a new setting in the video prompt. The docs say the pins should share a scene.
- One camera move at 1080p: do not send `last_frame`. Send the approved still as `image` only, `resolution` `1080p`, `duration` 6, and one motion sentence. The end position is then a description, not a pin. If the end pose must match a second still, drop to the 720p pin path.
- If the man must change position, name his start place and his end place. A dolly or push-in, in the official examples, is a camera sentence. Nothing in the docs says that sentence also walks the subject.

### Gaps
- No official prompt sentence was found that says “seamless,” “perfect loop,” or “loop the action.” The loop is the duplicated frame.
- No official test was found of a camera verb that leaves the subject stuck. That failure mode was asked for and was not in the docs or the news posts.
- The May 2026 community note’s “one action per second” and “15 seconds loses the middle” were not confirmed against `grok-imagine-video-1.5`.

## How should a prompt lock this fictional character?

### Takeaway
Official position: prose creates the first still. A reference image locks the face across later clips. Text-to-video invents a first frame from the prompt and will not hold him. For this man, generate one canonical 9:16 still, then pass that file. Do not keep re-describing him, and do not rely on a prose bible alone.

### Cited Findings
- The image guide’s identity control is descriptive and in-prompt: name the subject first, give recognizable attributes and a count, and keep those words the same. It does not claim that a paragraph reproduces the same face on a later generation. Its persistence path is the edit: keep the same subject, angle, crop, and placement, and change one to three details. Pasting a full new prompt “restages the scene” and the subject drifts. — [Image Prompting Guide](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/images/prompting-guide)
- Video reference images are “useful for virtual try-on, product placement, and stories with recurring characters.” They “incorporate specific people, objects, clothing, or other visual elements without locking the first frame.” — [Reference-to-Video](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/reference-to-video)
- 31 July 2026 news: “Pass in a character image and a voice reference, and both hold — the same face and the same voice in every scene.” “Each reference image locks one thing in place — a face, a product, a location. Keep a character and swap the scene, keep the scene and swap the character, or hold both and change only the action.” — [Imagine Video 1.5 with References](https://x.ai/news/grok-imagine-video-1-5-references)
- Image-to-video locks the opening by a different mechanism: the still becomes the first frame. June 2026 preview text says the result “continues the original image rather than reinterpreting it” and “holds detail and lighting from the input frame.” That post does not say the face survives a long restaging inside the clip. — [Image-to-Video](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/image-to-video); [Grok Imagine 1.5 Preview](https://x.ai/news/grok-imagine-1-5)
- Text-to-video on 1.5 and 1.5-lite first renders a frame from the prompt, then animates it. That is a new face unless a reference image is also sent. — [Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview)
- Anecdotal, May 2026, same `imagine-gallery` tests, older slugs. The author ranks locks as: `n=4` only inside one image request, reference-to-video as the strongest cross-call video lock, image-to-video chains as continuous but drifting after several hops, and a multi-image edit that says “preserve” as the way to put a known person in a new place. The author writes that text-to-video does not carry identity into a later image-to-video clip even with the same prompt, and that changing aspect ratio mid-chain recomposes the frame and shifts identity. The same note says image-to-video “interprets” the still and is not a pixel copy. That last claim conflicts with the October 2026 wording that the still “becomes the first frame” and that `last_frame` and keyframes appear “exactly.” The May tests were not run on the October pin API. — [character-consistency.md](https://github.com/Kearney-Evan-MI/imagine-gallery/blob/0e3bc99a715ba90a800bbbf34ee2382edbfdf095/integrations/grok-imagine-toolkit/references/character-consistency.md); [known-quirks.md](https://github.com/Kearney-Evan-MI/imagine-gallery/blob/0e3bc99a715ba90a800bbbf34ee2382edbfdf095/integrations/grok-imagine-toolkit/references/known-quirks.md)

### Inferences
- Production lock for this fictional man: one approved still is the identity file. Every later still is an edit of that file. Every 1080p clip is image-to-video from a 9:16 still descended from that file. Every clip that must keep his face while changing the scene, without pinning frame one, sends that file as `<IMAGE_0>` on `grok-imagine-video-1.5` and accepts 720p. Do not type his description again unless the edit is missing a trait.
- A prose block repeated from scratch is how text-to-video builds a new first frame. It is not the lock. The name Smokrates stays out of the prompt so the model is not asked to letter it or to match a real person.
- Keep ratio at 9:16 from the first still onward. The community aspect-ratio warning matches the official fact that image-to-video copies the still’s ratio and that a mismatched pin would be a different composition.

### Gaps
- The docs do not say a reference image survives a prompt that also redescribes a different face. No official “prose versus reference” bake-off was found.
- Whether the October 1.5 first frame is pixel-identical, or only “the first frame” in the API’s sense, was not tested here. The May 2026 “interprets, doesn’t pixel-copy” note is the conflict, and it is anecdotal.
- Official multi-image stills do not document the `<IMAGE_0>` preserve prompt the community uses. Do not depend on those tokens for stills until a doc says them.

## What must stay out of the generated frames?

### Takeaway
Do not ask the model to paint SMOKRATES, a caption, a lower-third, a chart, or a counter. Draw those in code over a clean cream field. Object counts inside the prompt are different: the image guide wants them. Official docs never say that letters are garbled. A May 2026 community test says short labels can work and that body copy, captions, and precise diagrams do not.

### Cited Findings
- The image guide does not discuss typography. It does say an avoid list is a weak control, and that named counts are part of a successful still: “How many. Two mugs. One open notebook. One person.” A fix is judged on whether “the count matches.” — [Image Prompting Guide](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/images/prompting-guide)
- No video page found tells the model to render captions, lower-thirds, or charts. Video prompts in the docs describe shots, motion, and sound. — [Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview); [Video Generation](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation)
- Anecdotal, May 2026, `imagine-gallery` graphic-fallback note. The author writes that short signs and labels, “up to ~10 words,” rendered well enough to leave in the picture, and that body copy, multi-line layout, captions, and infographic labels degrade: wrapping, spacing, and legibility. The workaround they document is a background with “empty negative space” and type composited afterward, because fixing a letter means regenerating the picture. The same author’s prompting notes say precise mathematical or scientific diagrams fail, and that quoted short text on a named surface can work. They also recommend JSON-like layout keys for posters. That JSON shape conflicts with the 8 October 2026 image guide, which says to write prose for one photograph. Do not use the JSON shape. — [graphic-fallback-patterns.md](https://github.com/Kearney-Evan-MI/imagine-gallery/blob/0e3bc99a715ba90a800bbbf34ee2382edbfdf095/integrations/grok-imagine-toolkit/references/graphic-fallback-patterns.md); [prompting-tips.md](https://github.com/Kearney-Evan-MI/imagine-gallery/blob/0e3bc99a715ba90a800bbbf34ee2382edbfdf095/integrations/grok-imagine-toolkit/references/prompting-tips.md); [Image Prompting Guide](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/images/prompting-guide)

### Inferences
- Keep out of the Grok frame, and draw in the editor: the word SMOKRATES, any other exact brand line, captions, lower-thirds, charts, and on-screen numerals or counters. The reason to keep the brand word out is not a documented letter bug. It is that the word must be identical on every post, and both the official edit rule and the community note say a regeneration changes the picture around the lettering.
- Keep in the prompt: “one” man, and any other object count that must be visible. Dropping counts would violate the image guide. “Counts” here means numbers painted in the frame, not the count of subjects.
- The bare cream panel is the handover to code. It is a region of the picture, described positively, not a negative prompt that says “no text.”
- Charts and lower-thirds were not found as official failure cases. Treat that ban as a studio rule plus the community diagram and caption warning, not as an xAI specification.

### Gaps
- No October 2026 xAI page was found that measures text accuracy on `grok-imagine-image-2.0` or on `grok-imagine-video-1.5`.
- The community claim that short text is reliable is five months old, anecdotal, and contradicted in spirit by their own advice to composite anything that must stay editable. It was not retested for this note.
