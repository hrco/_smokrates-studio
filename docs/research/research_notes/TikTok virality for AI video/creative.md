# TikTok creative craft for a motion-graphics and AI-clip pipeline (as of 2026-10-13)

Scope is organic short-form craft: hook, structure, length, on-screen text, audio, faceless formats, share vs like, retention-killers, and named accounts. Distribution, hashtags, and posting cadence are out of scope except where a study uses them as a control.

How to read the sources, before the questions:

- TikTok has not published an optimal length, a first-3-second threshold, or a completion-rate cutoff. A 2026 review of TikTok's public docs says every "21–34 seconds" and "70% completion unlocks distribution" figure is a third-party estimate or an invention. — [Socialrails](https://socialrails.com/blog/best-tiktok-video-length-maximum-engagement)
- The only large 2025–2026 measurements of what was actually posted are observational, and they measure different populations. They conflict. None of them is a public retention curve or a randomized hook test. viral.app says outright that watch time and retention are not visible on other people's videos, and that there is no reliable public label for AI video. — [viral.app, 3.3M videos, Oct 2025–Aug 2026](https://viral.app/blog/insights/short-form-video-trends)
- Vendor "statistics" pages that quote 70–85% 3-second retention, 2.2x view multipliers, or a 72% vs 24% platform average (TTS Vibes, CutFast, Viral Hook Writer, Wavevision, FacelessGenie, HookStudio's save×3/share×5 formula) are not used below. The numbers are not traceable to a method.

## What does the first 1–3 seconds need to do, and which hook types show up in high-completion or high-share videos?

### Takeaway
The first sentence has to create a specific curiosity gap, a stake, or a contradiction before any setup. In the best 2026 observational sets, mystery questions ("Investigator") and bold claims ("Hot Take") are the openings that scale; story setup ("so the other day") does not. There is still no public retention curve that proves a 3-second threshold, and "you" callouts beat "I" in one creator corpus while an academic sample of 46,605 hooks says the opposite.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok's own 2020 For You explanation, still the canonical public description and not updated with 2025–2026 weights: recommendations use likes, shares, comments, follows, and content created; video information includes captions, sounds, and hashtags. "A strong indicator of interest, such as whether a user finishes watching a longer video from beginning to end, would receive greater weight than a weak indicator, such as whether the video's viewer and creator are both in the same country." Replays are named as an early learning signal for new users. Follower count is not a direct factor. This does not say the decision happens at 3 seconds, and it does not rank shares above likes. — [TikTok Newsroom, 18 Jun 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- Historical ads research, not organic, dated Nov 2022 and citing 2020–2021 studies: "The most valuable part of a video is the first 2 seconds for ad recall, and the first 2.5 seconds for awareness." The same piece says those seconds should not be packed with logos and CTAs, because the job is to hook through entertainment. A title in the opening is associated with +48% brand recall, +24% perceived uniqueness, +45% brand affinity. A CTA in the opening frame is associated with +44% conversion versus a CTA later inside the first 2 seconds (US SMB study, Lumen, 2021). Surprising transitions: +53% brand recall and +19% consideration; more seamless transitions: +14% view time. — [TikTok Creative Center, "The Power of Creative Elements," 28 Nov 2022](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/Power_Creative_Elements/pc/en)
- Academic preprint, not peer-reviewed, August 2025: 46,605 TikTok opening lines across 20+ categories. Emotional hooks are rare (about 3.2% of posts) but drive proportionally higher comments and shares; any like advantage is modest. Curiosity gaps and urgency statements outperform direct address and "how to" phrasing across most categories. Optimal hook length swings from about 9 words in news to about 90 in entertainment. The strongest predictors are content category, hook length, urgency cues, and emotional richness, not a single phrase. Five clusters; "Meta/Community Humor" and "Fast & CTA-Heavy" predict engagement best. — [Mishra, SocArXiv, 6 Aug 2025](https://osf.io/preprints/socarxiv/rj2mz_v1)
- The Content Labs, 10,042 short-form videos (TikTok, Reels, Shorts), 103 creators, tagged 26 Apr 2026 and split into under 100k / 100k–1M / 1M+. Share of the tier using each opening:
  - Investigator (a specific mystery: "Did you know that snowplows are banned in the NFL?"): 22.5% → 33.7% → 31.5%. The only archetype that scales at every step, and the plurality of both hit tiers.
  - Hot Take (bold opinion as fact): 13.3% → 18.7% → 11.9%. Peaks in the solid-hit tier and loses share at 1M+.
  - Magician ("watch this" / "wait for it" / impossible reveal): 5.7% → 5.3% → 10.3%. Doubles its share only in the mega-viral tier.
  - Teacher ("let me show you how"): 18.2% → 9.8% → 13.2%. Collapses in the middle.
  - Contrarian ("everyone says X, actually Y"): 16.0% → 13.4% → 10.9%. Slow decline.
  - Experimenter ("I tried X for 30 days"): 3.3% → 1.9% → 1.3%. Stops showing up.
  - FaceTime Energy ("hey, what's up"): flat and low, about 3–5%.
  Named examples they tag as Investigator or Magician include @Ev Hand, "Did you know that snowplows are banned in the NFL?" at 2.84M views (YouTube Short, voiceover), and @Evan Hand Sports, "Nobody fumbled the bag harder than the French gymnastics team," at 17.7M (voiceover + b-roll). Corpus is creators they already analyze, not a random sample of TikTok, and the same account family is over-represented in the examples. — [The Content Labs, 26 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/10k-vs-1m-views-data-study)
- Same lab, separate cut of 3,997 videos, 109 creators, June 2025–April 2026 (about 47% TikTok, 53% Reels). Hot Take and Investigator average about 140k views. Story openings ("so the other day," "let me tell you about") average 7,127 views, about 20x lower. Proof Drop openings (a chart, a receipt, a specific number, "screenshot this") average 1,761 saves per video, the highest save count of the hook types in that cut. These are means, so a few hits move them. The "algorithm decides in 1.5 seconds" line in that article is the author's interpretation, not a TikTok disclosure. — [The Content Labs, 20 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-goes-viral-in-2026-data-study)
- Same lab, summaries published 20 Aug 2026 on a 14,424-video / 355-account set, scored as a multiple of each account's own typical views (better than raw averages, but the full method pages were not retrievable; the blog index is the source):
  - Question-phrased hooks 10.08x vs statements 7.04x (about 43% higher).
  - Hooks aimed at "you/your" 9.62x vs "I/my/me" 6.18x (about 56% higher).
  - A number in the hook: 7.77x vs 7.42x without, a 5% gap; money numbers performed worst.
  - Spoken/written hook length of 9–12 words beat other lengths; the index calls ultra-short hooks "the most overrated advice in short-form."
  - A separate grade of 2,485 hooks: A-tier hooks ran 10.14x the account's typical views, B and C about 1.7x, D/F below the account's own average. The rubric itself is not public in the index. — [The Content Labs index, 20 Aug 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog)
- A linguistic teardown of 165 viral scripts from the same lab (28 Apr 2026) reports more questions in higher view tiers (1.16 questions per script under 100k, 1.68 at 100k–1M, 1.84 at 1M+). The first word "Hey" had the highest median views in that set (they also print 61,029 in the closer and 39,450 in the table; the two figures disagree on the page). "What," "Did," and "Why" sit in their top 10 openers. "Okay" is the lowest. This contradicts the guru rule "never open with hey," at least for face creators whose "hey" is immediately followed by a claim. — [The Content Labs, 28 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-viral-scripts-actually-say)
- Comment rate is a different ranking from views. On videos with at least 5,000 views in that lab's set, median comment rate is highest for Hot Take (0.135%) and FaceTime Energy (0.133%), then Contrarian (0.116%). Investigator, the view-scaling hook, is mid-pack at 0.084%. Experimenter is last at 0.058%. People watch a curiosity gap and move on; they argue with an opinion. — [The Content Labs, 28 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/watchable-vs-talkable-comments-data-study)

### Inferences
- For this pipeline, the executable hooks are Investigator (one specific unexplained fact), Contrarian or Hot Take (one sentence that contradicts a default belief), and Proof Drop (the number or diagram is on screen at frame 1). All three are text-and-voice, not a face.
- "Wait for it" only shows up disproportionately at the 1M tier when there is a visual promise. A verbal wait with no change in frame is the failure mode, not the pattern.
- Identity callout ("if you…") is contested. Use it as a test, not a default. Mishra's larger sample says urgency and a curiosity gap beat second-person address.
- Do not write "so basically" or "let me tell you." Start on the claim.
- Do not treat 70% 3-second hold, or any other round retention threshold, as a TikTok rule. It is not in the public docs.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 source here publishes a retention curve, a 1-second vs 3-second drop, or an A/B test of hook wordings on organic TikTok. viral.app states that retention is not publicly measurable.
- The full Content Labs articles behind the 14,424-video word-count, question, "you," and number tests were not opened (index summaries only; a guessed URL 404'd).
- Pattern-interrupt as a visual, separate from the words, is not quantified outside 2021 ads studies on transitions.
- "Wait for it" is an archetype label in one corpus, not a counted phrase.

## How should a video be structured after the hook?

### Takeaway
What is measured is the shape of the promise, not a second-by-second payoff map. Curiosity has to be answered, a "wait for it" has to pay off visually, and in one 2026 creator corpus an implied ending beat a spoken "follow me." Series, recurring characters, and the best second to land the payoff are not in any dataset retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- In the 10,042-video tier study, the formats that gain share as views climb are skit (2.6% of the long tail → 9.3% of 1M+) and voiceover + b-roll (13.1% → 10.1% → 20.4%, a jump only at the top). Talking head falls from 48.7% to 33.2% and is still the largest single format at every tier. Greenscreen is a 100k–1M specialty (8.5% → 18.5% → 6.4%) and then caps. Montage almost disappears (1.1% → 0.3%). — [The Content Labs, 26 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/10k-vs-1m-views-data-study)
- Emotional register shifts with the same tiers. Humor rises steadily (7.4% → 10.3% → 16.0%). Trust/utility collapses (6.6% → 2.8% → 2.9%). Fear doubles at the top (2.9% → 6.7%). Curiosity is the volume engine but peaks in the middle (39.5% → 45.4% → 30.8%). Outrage peaks in the middle (8.6%) and eases at the top (6.4%). The lab's reading: "give value" is a long-tail strategy; humor is the only emotion whose share keeps rising. — [The Content Labs, 26 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/10k-vs-1m-views-data-study)
- Idea type, in the 3,997-video cut: commentary beats a straight lesson. The article says myth-busting, dominant in 2023–2024, averages about 8k views in this set, and promotional videos average about 26k. An educational-plus-commentary hybrid is described at about 124k average views. Exact cell sizes for idea type are not in the fetched text. — [The Content Labs, 20 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-goes-viral-in-2026-data-study)
- Endings: in that same cut, implied CTA averages 138,650 views (485 videos) versus no CTA at 99,432 (243) and a direct CTA ("follow," "link," "comment X") at 72,379 (205). The one video they hold up as hitting every winning tag is a 174.6-second greenscreen commentary from @ev_handd at 750,062 views that ends on an unresolved situation rather than an ask. Direct-CTA depression is their interpretation. — [The Content Labs, 20 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-goes-viral-in-2026-data-study)
- Of 63 faceless videos over 1M in a 7,431-video companion study, almost all are a single narrator over relevant b-roll, not a montage, not text-only, not a screen recording. Four creators account for most of those 63 hits. Niches they name: sports news, history facts, food and lifestyle, viral-moment commentary, POV. The hooks are one sentence in the first second. — [The Content Labs, 26 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/faceless-vs-face-on-camera-data-study)
- List and proof structures still produce saves even when they do not dominate the viral tier. Proof Drop is flat across view tiers (about 4–5% of each) but leads saves. A face-led list example they cite: @leonidkimmd, "Top 5 Foods Lower Blood Pressure Naturally," 6.8M views. That is a person on camera, not a faceless graphic. — [The Content Labs, 26 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/10k-vs-1m-views-data-study); [20 Apr 2026 cut](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-goes-viral-in-2026-data-study)
- Socialinsider's reading of its brand data, explicitly an interpretation: a video around 30 seconds is long enough to set up a hook and deliver a payoff while the viewer is still there, which is why that band wins engagement rate, while a 2-minute video can accumulate more total watch time if the material is a real tutorial or story. Not a timed-payoff experiment. — [Socialinsider, 10 Jul 2026, updated 21 Jul 2026](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/how-long-are-tiktok-videos/)
- Loop craft, opinion only, 4 Oct 2026: an editor describes a 5.7-second clip of a faceless grey mannequin throwing a jab, cross, and hook on black, with an on-screen counter incrementing each punch, so the viewer counts and the loop has already replayed. He says the hard production problem was character consistency across a cut, solved by specifying no face. No account name and no view count. — [@sulfurscales, 4 Oct 2026](https://x.com/sulfurscales/status/2106719924001575108)

### Inferences
- A producible structure for this pipeline is three beats inside 15–40 seconds: claim, one piece of evidence (diagram, number, generated cutaway), payoff that answers the claim. That matches Investigator plus a Proof Drop, which are the two faceless-friendly shapes.
- For a loop, the last frame has to be the first frame, and the video needs a count, a transformation, or a punchline that is funnier the second time. Under 10 seconds is the natural length for that job. There is no dataset here that measures replay rate by ending type.
- Myth-bust and pure "how to" are weaker as the container than commentary that happens to teach. Put the correction inside a contradiction, not a lesson plan.
- A direct "follow for part 2" is associated with lower views in one corpus. If a series is used, the episode has to be complete. Recurring-character series are a production choice, not a measured growth lever.
- Rant, storytime, and demo rankings are not cleanly separated in these datasets. Storytime as an opening loses. Storytime as the body, after a claim, is not isolated.

### Gaps
- No source gives the second at which the payoff should land, or a measured lift from a looped ending versus a hard end.
- No 2025–2026 measurement of series, recurring characters, or episode numbering on completion or follows.
- The 8k-view myth-bust figure and the commentary ranking are means from one non-random corpus, with cell sizes missing for idea type.

## What video lengths win in 2026, and did 60–180 seconds stick?

### Takeaway
The shift to long video did not stick in what people post. Across 3.3 million public videos from October 2025 to August 2026 the median TikTok is 16 seconds, two-thirds of short-form posts are under 30 seconds, and the same accounts' median length moved only from 19.5 to 20 seconds. Longer videos break out to a million views more often, but inside the same account they get only about 6% more views than that account's shorter posts. Among videos that are already popular, 16–30 seconds still has the highest median views. "60–180 seconds is the new default" describes a minority of uploads and a disputed view effect, not the center of the platform.

### Cited Findings
- viral.app, 3.3 million public videos with at least one view, posted 1 Oct 2025–31 Aug 2026 by about 81,000 accounts that brands, agencies, and creators track. Views as of 1 Oct 2026. Medians, same-account comparisons where noted.
  - All TikToks: median length 16 seconds. Share of TikToks: under 10s 26.9%, 10–29s 42.1%, 30–59s 20.3%, 1–3 min 10.1%. About 1 in 10 TikToks is longer than a minute. Between 66% and 74% of videos on TikTok, Reels, Shorts, and Facebook run under 30 seconds.
  - Same accounts, at least 5 videos in each half: median TikTok length 19.5 seconds (Oct 2025–Mar 2026) to 20 seconds (Apr–Aug 2026), about 5,100 accounts. Length did not change.
  - Share of videos that hit 10x their own account's median views: under 10s 4.5%, 10–29s 4.7%, 30–59s 5.7%, 1–3 min 7.5%.
  - 1–3 minute TikToks reached 1 million views 4 times as often as TikToks under 10 seconds (1.85% vs 0.43%). Inside the same account the effect is small: 1.06 times the median views of that account's videos under a minute, and better in 57% of about 4,300 accounts.
  - They could not measure watch time or completion. — [viral.app](https://viral.app/blog/insights/short-form-video-trends)
- Socialinsider, more than 6 million TikToks from brand accounts with an active presence, January–June 2026. Not creators, and the absolute view levels are low.
  - Engagement rate: 0–15s 5.90%, 15–30s 6.00% (peak), 30–60s 4.20% (floor), 60–90s 4.80%, 90–120s 5.15%, 120–180s 5.50%, over 180s 5.90%.
  - Median views: 1–15s 1,274, 15–30s 1,000, 30–60s 2,200, 60–90s 7,200, 90–120s 9,620, 120–180s 11,136 (peak), over 180s 10,150.
  - Their explanation, tied back to the 2020 newsroom post: short videos win engagement because people finish them and then like or comment; long videos win views because total watch time, not completion percentage, is what gets further distribution. That split of the algorithm is their inference. TikTok has not published those two scoring logics. — [Socialinsider, Jul 2026](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/how-long-are-tiktok-videos/)
- Revid index of 2.39 million TikToks with at least 1,000 likes, pulled 3 Oct 2026. This is already-popular video, so medians sit in the tens or hundreds of thousands and must not be compared with viral.app's typical-post medians. Medians are estimated from view thresholds.
  - Under 15s: median about 106k, 22% passed 1M.
  - 16–30s: about 141k, 25% passed 1M. Highest on both.
  - 31–60s: about 120k, 23% passed 1M.
  - 61–90s: about 102k, 20%.
  - 91s–3 min: about 85k, 18%.
  - 3–10 min: about 91k, 18%.
  - The mix of popular posts is getting longer even though the conditional median is not: share of this index running over one minute was 22% of 2023 posts (216,297 videos), 29% of 2024 (826,324), 32% of 2025 (1,105,678), and 45% of 2026 posts indexed so far (56,504, partial year, treat as early). — [Revid, 3 Oct 2026](https://www.revid.ai/short-form-video-statistics)
- The Content Labs 3,997-video cut reports the opposite shape on means: 90s+ averages 170,228 views (777 videos), 61–90s averages 126,299 (1,880), 31–60s averages 27,797 (760), 16–30s averages 31,998 (319), 0–15s averages 5,406 (261). They call this 31x and recommend 60–90 seconds as the sweet spot, arguing a 90-second video at 70% completion produces more watch time than a finished 10-second video. The 70% figure is hypothetical, not observed. This conflicts with viral.app's within-account 1.06x and with Revid's popular-index peak at 16–30s. Likely confound: in their creator set, long videos are a different kind of video (sports commentary, greenscreen), not length alone. — [The Content Labs, 20 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-goes-viral-in-2026-data-study)
- TikTok's public docs, per a September 2025 review that was still being cited as the skeptical baseline in 2026: TikTok has never published an optimal length. The "21 to 34 seconds" entertainment sweet spot repeated on blogs is not a TikTok figure. Upload ceilings are not the same thing as a performance band. — [Socialrails, 2 Sep 2025](https://socialrails.com/blog/best-tiktok-video-length-maximum-engagement)

### Inferences
- Match length to the job, and do not adopt 60–180 seconds as the house style.
  - One visual gag, one satisfying action, one loop: under 10 seconds, often 5–8. This is a third of TikToks. It is the weakest breakout band in viral.app and the right band when the replay is the product.
  - One claim, one proof, one payoff: 15–30 seconds. Peak engagement rate on brand posts, peak median views and million-view rate inside the popular index, and the length the median account actually posts.
  - A real sequence (multi-step demo, a story with a turn): 45–90 seconds, and only if every beat is doing work. Past that, views can rise and completion will fall; the within-account view gain for going to 1–3 minutes is about 6%.
- The pipeline can hit all three bands. It should not pad a 20-second idea to two minutes to chase the Socialinsider or Content Labs means. Those means are not within-account effects.
- Education and product demos are the jobs that can earn the longer band. Entertainment and a single diagram usually cannot.

### Gaps
- No completion rate by length from TikTok or from a public dataset in 2025–2026. viral.app says it cannot see watch time. Guru tables that quote 81% completion under 10 seconds and 23% over 90 seconds are unsourced and unused here.
- Buffer's March 2025 analysis of 1.1 million videos (often quoted as +43% reach for posts over 60 seconds) was not retrieved from Buffer. Left out rather than cited secondhand.
- The 2026 slice of Revid's "over one minute" share is 56,504 videos, early in the year, and the index only contains posts with 1,000+ likes. It is a composition shift among hits, not evidence that a random long upload outperforms a short one.

## What is the role of on-screen text, captions, spoken hooks, and original sound versus trending sound?

### Takeaway
On-screen text is a 2021 ads finding that still matches how the craft works: a short title in the first seconds lifts recall, and 5–10 words a second beat other densities in that ads study. In 2026 organic data, original sound is now the majority of TikToks and, inside the same account, licensed or widely used sounds do not get more views. Trending audio still matters when the sound is the format. It does not matter as a reach hack for an explainer.

### Cited Findings
- Original sound is rising and is not a penalty. Same ~3,800 TikTok accounts: 65.4% of videos used a sound TikTok flags as original in Oct 2025–Mar 2026, and 70.1% in Apr–Aug 2026. Accounts using original sound on 90%+ of videos rose from 27.8% to 33.3%. Across all TikToks in the period, 71% of videos and 53% of slideshows used original sound. Inside the same account, licensed music got 1.01 times the median views of original sound (~11,000 accounts); for slideshows, 0.99 times. Sounds used by 100+ tracked accounts hit 10x the account median in 5.3% of cases, versus 4.9% for sounds no other tracked account used. 46% of TikToks used a sound nobody else in the panel used. — [viral.app](https://viral.app/blog/insights/short-form-video-trends)
- TikTok's recommendation system reads sounds and captions as video information, alongside user actions. Hiding a sound is a negative signal a viewer can send. Historical, 2020, and still the public statement. — [TikTok Newsroom, 18 Jun 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- Historical ads research, Nov 2022 write-up of 2020–2021 studies, about 4,000 ads coded in the US, Canada, and the UK: 93% of users spend time on TikTok with sound on. 15% of users are more likely to skip a sound-off ad. Music is in 63% of ads, voiceover in 39%, text-to-speech in 20%. Text boxes are in 86% of ads; text overlays in 71%. Captions present: +58% recall, +25% uniqueness, +95% brand affinity, +31% likeability. A title at the start: +48% brand recall. "Fewer and sooner": 5–10 words per second was the text density with a +2.1x awareness lift versus other timings. Voiceover drove 71% higher recognition than ASMR in one US audio study. Original audio: a VidMob analysis found +52% awareness versus registered tracks. Recognizable music did better on likeability (44% more likeable than instrumentals or brand-made music, EU) and showed up in 80% of top awareness-and-intent creatives in a separate meta-analysis. Custom, unrecognizable music did better on brand recognition (+34%) and aided recall (+78%) in a US study. The piece's own conclusion: recognizable music for likeability and lower-funnel, original or custom audio for recall and ownership. These are ads, not For You organic posts, and they are four years old. — [TikTok Creative Center, 28 Nov 2022](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/Power_Creative_Elements/pc/en)
- Hook length in words, from the Content Labs index (method page not opened): 9–12 word hooks beat other lengths on a multiple of the account's typical views, across 14,424 videos. Ultra-short hooks underperformed that band. Mishra, on a different sample, finds the best length is the niche's length: ~9 words in news, ~90 in entertainment. A single word-count rule fights the category effect. — [The Content Labs index, 20 Aug 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog); [Mishra, Aug 2025](https://osf.io/preprints/socarxiv/rj2mz_v1)
- A May 2026 Content Labs ranking of 749 videos versus the prior 90 days is a small, fast-moving cut, useful only as a fatigue signal: "Pull Them In" (open a mystery) engagement 4.71%, down 1.28 points; Hot Take 4.70%, down 1.90 points. Their note: generic "nobody told you" mysteries are tiring, and the mystery has to be specific. Opinion-adjacent because the window is one month and the lab sells the tagger. — [The Content Labs, 30 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/tiktok-hooks-trending-may-2026)
- Sound lifespan, vendor, not a view test: Virlo's October 2026 trend note says original-audio trends tend to peak in five to ten days and commercial tracks in two to four weeks, attributing the window to SocialPilot's September data. The post itself is a costume-trend roundup (Two Fishes, Ramalama Costume Walk, Patient Zero wedding edits). Treat the lifespan as secondhand. — [Virlo, 5 Oct 2026](https://virlo.ai/blog/whats-trending-on-tiktok)
- TikTok's own older framework, restated by a 2026 secondary source: "Trend Moments" (popular sounds, hashtags, formats) last days to weeks; the "48 to 72 hours" figure common on blogs is not in a TikTok publication. The underlying report is the 2023 What's Next edition, so this is historical framing, not a 2026 measurement. — [TTS Vibes citing TikTok What's Next 2023; the lifespan claim is the part that matches TikTok's published tier names, and the rest of that site is not used](https://insights.ttsvibes.com/tiktok-sound-adoption-speed-statistics/)

### Inferences
- Default to original voiceover plus a designed score or no music. In the 2026 panel that is the majority behavior and it does not cost views. It is also the version that can be searched, duetted, and reused, which a licensed bed cannot become.
- Use a trending sound only when the edit is a participation in that sound (the joke, the cut, the lyric timing). Laying a chart-topper under a kinetic explainer is the case viral.app says does not pay.
- Put the hook sentence on screen in the first second, in the upper-middle safe area, large enough to read with sound off. Keep the density near a short phrase, not a paragraph. The 5–10 words/second ads figure is a ceiling for a spoken line being reinforced, not permission for a wall of text.
- Spoken hook and on-screen hook should be the same sentence. The ads literature treats them as one message. A spoken hook with unrelated text splits attention; that specific failure is not A/B tested in the sources above.
- Kinetic type is a native fit: the text is the visual hook, which is the layer this pipeline controls exactly. Word-by-word captions on a voiceover are supported by the old caption-recall numbers and by sound-off viewing, not by a 2026 organic completion test.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 organic test of captions vs no captions, Hormozi-style word-by-word vs a lower-third, or sound-off viewing share. The "80% watch with sound off" and "captions add 32% completion" figures circulating in 2026 could not be traced to a primary study. The TikTok-owned figure that does exist is the opposite direction and old: 93% of users spend time with sound on, and sound-off ads are skipped more.
- No measurement of whether original voiceover helps TikTok search, versus caption keywords. The newsroom says captions and sounds are inputs. It does not quantify search.
- The English TikTok Next 2026 report was not retrieved. A Chilean wire on 13 Oct 2026 describes it as a cultural brief (reality, off-path discovery, emotional ROI), not a craft or audio study.

## Which formats stay viral and can be made with no human on camera?

### Takeaway
Two datasets disagree because they sample different ends of the distribution, and both can be true. In a creator corpus that includes flops, the typical face video gets about 2.4x the median views of the typical faceless video on TikTok, and almost every faceless million-view hit is voiceover over b-roll. In an index that only keeps posts with 1,000+ likes, faceless videos that already worked have a higher median than person-on-camera videos, and animation is liked and shared more than photoreal AI. The formats this pipeline can actually own are voiceover plus generated or designed b-roll, kinetic type, a proof graphic, a short satisfying loop, and a photo sequence. Greenscreen, duet, stitch, and skit want a person.

### Cited Findings
- Faceless vs face, long tail included. 7,431 videos, 96 creators, TikTok 3,562 / Instagram 3,485 / Shorts 384, posted May 2020–April 2026 and dominated by the last 18 months, analyzed 26 Apr 2026. Face = talking head, greenscreen, skit, interview, vlog. Faceless = voiceover + b-roll, montage, text overlay, slideshow, screen recording.
  - Median views: face 12,503 vs faceless 6,630 (1.9x). Mean views go the other way (faceless 248,789 vs face 193,091) because of a few mega-hits. The lab says to trust the median for "what the next post does."
  - TikTok specifically: face median 9,856 vs faceless 4,051 (2.4x), the widest gap of the three platforms.
  - 1M+ rate: faceless 4.44% (63 of 1,418) vs face 2.86% (172 of 6,013). 100k+ rate: face 22.4% vs faceless 17.8%. Of the 1,598 videos over 100k, 1,345 (84%) are face.
  - Hook penalty for taking the face away, median views: Magician 26,700 vs 5,962 (4.5x face). FaceTime Energy 7,093 vs 2,309 (3.1x). Investigator 27,775 vs 10,102 (2.7x). Hot Take 21,572 vs 8,725 (2.5x). Teacher 5,668 vs 4,313 (1.3x). Fortune Teller 8,789 vs 7,018 (1.3x). Proof Drop 9,594 vs 8,735 (1.1x). Contrarian is the only flip: faceless 10,526 vs face 8,678 (1.2x faceless).
  - The 63 faceless 1M-view videos are almost all voiceover + b-roll. Text-only and pure montage do not appear in that tier. — [The Content Labs, 26 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/faceless-vs-face-on-camera-data-study)
- Format share as videos scale, same lab's 10,042-video cut: voiceover + b-roll is 20.4% of the 1M tier versus 13.1% of the long tail. Skit triples its share and is not faceless. Greenscreen spikes at 100k–1M (18.5%) then falls to 6.4% of 1M+. Montage dies. Talking head remains the volume default. — [The Content Labs, 26 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/10k-vs-1m-views-data-study)
- Popular-only index, opposite headline, selection bias stated by the authors. Revid, 2.39 million TikToks with 1,000+ likes, 3 Oct 2026, AI-classified subject:
  - Animal: 57,184 videos, median about 273k.
  - Faceless (no person): 160,703 videos, median about 198k.
  - Object: 266,368 videos, median about 143k.
  - Group: 157,161 videos, median about 140k.
  - One person: 1,407,595 videos, median about 105k.
  - Faceless median is 1.9x the one-person median inside this already-liked set. That does not contradict the Content Labs median. It says faceless videos that clear 1,000 likes are bigger than person videos that clear the same bar, while the typical faceless attempt still loses.
  - Handheld median about 141k vs selfie about 67k. Selfie had the highest likes per view (9.3%).
  - On 21,037 videos with a production-format label: green-screen memes median about 204k and street interviews about 200k, versus talking head about 91k (about 2.2x). Street interviews and green-screen memes are human formats.
  - Storytelling is the most common type among classified popular videos (36%). Challenges (median about 227k) and comedy (about 194k) get more views per video. — [Revid, 3 Oct 2026](https://www.revid.ai/short-form-video-statistics)
- AI footage vs real vs animation, Revid, videos posted May 2025–September 2026, snapshot 15 Sep 2026. Vision labels on 292,784 TikToks in a keyword-sampled, viral-skewed index (9,940 AI, 252,107 real, 16,534 animation, 14,203 mixed). Classifier errors are admitted (photoreal AI can be tagged real).
  - Mean views: AI 1.4M, animation 797.6k, mixed 713.8k, real 661.4k (AI 2.1x real).
  - Estimated median: AI 151.7k, animation 121.3k, mixed 87.3k, real 67.3k (AI about 2.3x real).
  - Share passing 1M: about 20% of AI vs about 10% of real (FAQ: 19.5% vs 9.5%). Animation about 14%, mixed about 11%.
  - Like rate (likes per view): animation 8.4%, real 7.7%, mixed 6.3%, AI 3.9%. AI is last.
  - Share rate: animation 1.04%, mixed 1.01%, AI 0.94%, real 0.87%.
  - AI's share of tagged videos in this index peaked at 4.3% in June 2025 (2,956 of 68,125) and fell to 0.5% by December 2025 (34 of 6,633). Late months have fewer indexed videos, so the tail is noisy.
  - Their reading: people watch AI because it is impossible or uncanny, and they forward it, but a like is an endorsement they withhold. Animation gets both the views and the affection. — [Revid, 15 Sep 2026](https://www.revid.ai/case-studies/ai-videos-vs-real)
- Photo carousels, full-population not just hits. viral.app: 18% of TikTok posts in Oct 2025–Aug 2026 were slideshows (about 310,000 vs 1.4 million videos). Median views 880 vs 866 for video. Inside ~4,100 accounts with at least 5 of each, slideshows got 1.02x the median views of that account's videos, and won in 51% of accounts. They reached 1M views 1 in 290 times, versus 1 in 120 for video. Versus the same account's videos: +18% saves per 1,000 views, −13% shares, −12% likes. Same-account share of posts that are slideshows was flat (14.6% to 15.4%). — [viral.app](https://viral.app/blog/insights/short-form-video-trends)
- Separate 3,997-video means, for context only: greenscreen averages about 150k views, plain talking head about 56k, montage about 12k. Greenscreen here means a person standing in front of an article or clip. — [The Content Labs, 20 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-goes-viral-in-2026-data-study)

### Inferences
What this pipeline can execute, mapped to the evidence:

- Voiceover + designed or generated b-roll, one narrator, one niche. This is the only faceless container that shows up in the million-view tier of the creator corpus. Best hooks for it: Contrarian, Proof Drop, Teacher, a prediction. Avoid Magician and "hey friend" unless the picture itself is the trick.
- Kinetic typography and diagram explainers. Proof Drop is the hook with the smallest face penalty and the highest saves. A chart, a receipt, a before/after built in motion graphics is the native version. Text-only, with no visual change, does not appear among their faceless million-view hits.
- Short satisfying loops and object or process shots (the punch count, a mechanism assembling, a number ticking). Object-focused videos are a large class of already-popular TikToks (median about 143k in the liked index). The mannequin loop is a circulated craft opinion, not a measured account.
- Animation and diagrammatic characters rather than photoreal people. Animation has the highest like rate and share rate of the four visual origins. Photoreal AI gets watched and forwarded and liked least. If a character is needed, a non-human or a diagram beats a synthetic face. The X note that face identity drifted until the prompt said "no face" matches that constraint.
- Photo carousels for utility (a list someone saves). They match video on typical views and win saves. They rarely become the million-view post. Remotion can render them as a sequence or as a posted slideshow.
- Screen-recording-style UI demos are plausible as motion graphics of an interface. They are not in the faceless million-view set, so treat them as a utility format, not a viral format.

What it should not pretend to make:

- Greenscreen commentary, street interviews, duets, and stitches. The view numbers attached to those formats belong to a human reacting to a real artifact.
- Skits. They scale in the tier study and require performance.
- Magician "wait for it" with nothing impossible in the frame. The face penalty there is 4.5x.
- A faceless account aimed at TikTok's median. The typical faceless post in the creator corpus does about 40% of the views of the typical face post on TikTok. The bet is a narrow niche and a repeatable voiceover format, not volume of generic AI clips.

### Gaps
- Revid's "faceless" and "green-screen meme" labels are model classifications on a liked-only index. Content Labs' labels are a rule tagger on a tracked-creator corpus. Neither is a random sample, and they are not directly comparable.
- No clean 2025–2026 count of kinetic-type, satisfying-process, or screen-recording posts and their view rates outside those buckets.
- Duet and stitch performance versus an original post was not found.
- Whether TikTok downranks undisclosed AI video is not knowable from public counts. Revid says its index cannot show suppression, and the AI videos it can see outperform on views. That is not proof of no suppression, because failed or removed videos never enter the index.

## What makes someone share or send a TikTok, rather than like it?

### Takeaway
A like is common and a share is rare, so they are not the same behavior. In the 2026 creator corpus, empathy produces the shares, trust and proof produce the saves, and fear and outrage produce the views and the arguments. People forward something that is about someone they know, or that makes the sender look like they noticed it. They bookmark the thing they might need. Likes track enjoyment and endorsement, which is exactly what photoreal AI is bad at.

### Cited Findings
- Scale of the two acts, typical posts, not hits. Same accounts, viral.app: likes per 1,000 views moved from 53.7 to 52.8 between the two 2026 halves; shares per 1,000 views from 1.57 to 1.48. A share is on the order of 30 times rarer than a like. Slideshows, versus the same account's videos: +18% saves per 1,000 views, −13% shares, −12% likes. Saving is the utility gesture; sharing is not. — [viral.app](https://viral.app/blog/insights/short-form-video-trends)
- Among already-popular TikToks, Revid's full index: 7.9% likes per view, 0.50% shares per view, 0.07% comments per view. Even among hits, likes dwarf shares by about 16x. — [Revid, 3 Oct 2026](https://www.revid.ai/short-form-video-statistics)
- Emotion versus the three acts, Content Labs, 3,997 videos, means, small cells for the top emotions:
  - Fear: 51 videos, 264,031 avg views, 480 avg shares, 393 avg saves. Wins views.
  - Empathy: 34 videos, 170,369 avg views, 2,139 avg shares, 975 avg saves. Wins shares, about 3x the next emotions.
  - Outrage: 85 videos, 168,502 avg views, 616 shares, 313 saves.
  - Curiosity: 664 videos, 98,145 views, 720 shares, 532 saves. The workhorse, not the peak.
  - Humor: 78 videos, 92,253 views, 645 shares, 276 saves.
  - Trust: 224 videos, 42,149 views, 691 shares, 1,294 saves. Wins saves, loses views.
  - Aspiration: 86 videos, 29,168 views, 255 shares, 151 saves.
  - Hope: 40 videos, 5,423 views, 91 shares, 39 saves. Worst on every column.
  Fear and empathy cells are small. Means, not medians. Mixed TikTok and Reels. — [The Content Labs, 20 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-goes-viral-in-2026-data-study)
- Hook type splits the same way. Proof Drop leads saves (1,761 avg in that cut). Hot Take leads comments (0.135% median comment rate) and does not keep scaling into the 1M tier. Investigator leads views and is only mid-pack on comments. Humor is the emotion whose share of videos rises all the way to the 1M tier (7.4% to 16.0%). Fear's share doubles at that tier (2.9% to 6.7%), consistent with "I have to send this warning." — [The Content Labs, 20 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-goes-viral-in-2026-data-study); [26 Apr 2026 tier study](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/10k-vs-1m-views-data-study); [28 Apr 2026 comment study](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/watchable-vs-talkable-comments-data-study)
- AI is the clean split between watching and endorsing. Like rate 3.9% on AI-tagged TikToks versus 7.7% on real and 8.4% on animation. Share rate 0.94% on AI versus 0.87% on real, so AI is slightly more forwarded than live footage and much less liked. Animation leads both. The authors' line is "people watch AI videos and forward them. They do not like them." — [Revid, 15 Sep 2026](https://www.revid.ai/case-studies/ai-videos-vs-real)
- Mishra, August 2025: emotional openings are 3.2% of hooks and punch above their weight on comments and shares, with only a modest like advantage. Social currency in the academic sample is tied to feeling, not to information density. — [Mishra, SocArXiv](https://osf.io/preprints/socarxiv/rj2mz_v1)
- TikTok's 2020 statement treats a like and a share as the same class of signal ("videos you like or share") and treats finishing a longer video as the stronger interest signal. It does not publish a 2026 reweight toward shares. Formulas that assign shares 5x the weight of completion are not TikTok's. — [TikTok Newsroom, 18 Jun 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)

### Inferences
- To be sent, the video needs a person the viewer already has in mind (empathy, "this is us," a relationship argument) or a stake they feel responsible for passing on (fear, a warning). A pure explainer gets saved, not sent. Build the save on purpose: one diagram, one number, a list that survives without the voiceover.
- To be liked, it has to feel like something the viewer will put their name on. Animation and a real joke clear that bar more often than a synthetic face. If the pipeline's clips look like stock AI people, expect forwards from novelty and weak likes.
- Outrage and hot takes buy comments, which are a different goal from shares. Useful when the point is argument. Costly if the niche cannot afford a polarized audience, and they have a ceiling in the tier data.
- Social currency, in-group identity, and "this is so me" are the right mechanisms, but they are inferred from the empathy and humor results, not from a 2026 survey that asked people why they hit send.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 survey of why people send a TikTok (identity vs utility vs emotion) was retrieved. The emotion table is a proxy.
- Saves are missing for most of the viral.app video rows; the slideshow save gap is the one place they could measure it. Revid's public index stats above do not break out saves.
- A 31-video vendor analysis (SocialGPT, November 2025) reports saves and shares correlating with views more than comments do. The sample is too small to use.
- TikTok has not said, in any page retrieved, that shares outrank likes in 2026.

## What visual and pacing mistakes kill retention in the first second?

### Takeaway
The measured failure is opening on setup instead of on the claim. Story frames average about 20x fewer views than a hot take or a mystery in one corpus, and a montage with no point of view fades out of the viral tier. The famous "never say hey," "never use a logo," and "AI faces die in second one" rules are either contradicted, old ads findings, or unmeasured. Empty time at the start is still the right thing to cut, because completion is the signal TikTok has actually named.

### Cited Findings
- Story setup as the first sentence averages 7,127 views versus about 140k for Hot Take and Investigator, in the 3,997-video cut. The losing lines they name are "so the other day," "let me tell you about," and "so basically." — [The Content Labs, 20 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-goes-viral-in-2026-data-study)
- Montage's share of videos falls from 1.1% of the long tail to 0.3% of the 1M tier, and the other cut puts montage means near 12k views against about 150k for greenscreen and 56k for talking head. A sequence of clips with no claim is the pacing failure they can see. — [The Content Labs, 26 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/10k-vs-1m-views-data-study); [20 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-goes-viral-in-2026-data-study)
- "Hey" is not, by itself, a killer in their linguistic set. It was the highest-median first word, which they explicitly say contradicts the usual advice. The working version is a greeting tied to a person and an immediate stake, not "hey guys" followed by a logo or a topic announcement. FaceTime Energy as a whole stays a small share of every view tier (about 3–5%) and loses 3.1x median views when the face is removed. For a faceless video, an empty greeting has nothing to attach to. — [The Content Labs, 28 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-viral-scripts-actually-say); [26 Apr 2026 faceless study](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/faceless-vs-face-on-camera-data-study)
- Logos and slow branded opens: the 2022 TikTok ads essay says the first 2 seconds matter most for recall and the first 2.5 for awareness, and that packing logos and CTAs into them fights the reason people watch. It also cites an internal 2021 study titled "Heterogeneous Treatment Effects of First 2s Logo on 2-second Video Completion Rate" without printing the result. Do not invent a percentage. Branding, in their words, should be subtle and entertainment comes first. A CTA card in the opening did raise conversion in the 2021 Lumen ads study (+44%), so "no text in second one" is wrong for ads and is a different question from a logo sting. — [TikTok Creative Center, 28 Nov 2022](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/Power_Creative_Elements/pc/en)
- Text density: the same ads piece says fewer and sooner, with 5–10 words per second as the awareness sweet spot, not a full paragraph. Organic proof that a "wall of text" causes a first-second swipe was not found. The Content Labs word-count summary (9–12 words for the hook, ultra-short overrated) is about the spoken or written hook line, not about simultaneous on-screen paragraphs. — [TikTok Creative Center, 28 Nov 2022](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/Power_Creative_Elements/pc/en); [The Content Labs index, 20 Aug 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog)
- No motion: not directly measured. The closest evidence is that surprising or seamless transitions raised recall and view time in 2021 ads research, and that montage without a developing claim loses. A static title card is the production version of the story-setup failure. — [TikTok Creative Center, 28 Nov 2022](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/Power_Creative_Elements/pc/en)
- Mismatched or generic audio: inside the same account in 2026, licensed vs original and viral vs unique sounds do not move views (1.01x, and 5.3% vs 4.9% breakout). A bad audio match is therefore not showing up as a reach killer in that panel. It can still make the edit feel like an ad. The 2021 figure that sound-off ads are skipped 15% more often is about silence, not about the wrong song. — [viral.app](https://viral.app/blog/insights/short-form-video-trends); [TikTok Creative Center, 28 Nov 2022](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/Power_Creative_Elements/pc/en)
- Synthetic faces: they are not shown to die in the first second. In the liked index they are watched more than real footage and liked about half as often (3.9% vs 7.7%). Distrust shows up as withheld endorsement, not as a published first-second drop. Uncanny or "stock" specifically was not isolated from other AI looks. — [Revid, 15 Sep 2026](https://www.revid.ai/case-studies/ai-videos-vs-real)
- Teacher and Contrarian hooks are described, in a strategy post from the same lab, as slower to "show their hand" than Proof Drop and Investigator, and as the archetypes most often stuck in a small initial audience (8,411 videos tagged; they claim a 2.6x gap between best and worst archetype on a stuck-rate). The post is more interpretive than the tier study. Use it as a pacing note: if the first sentence is "today I'm going to show you" or "most people think," the claim has not happened yet. — [The Content Labs, 17 Apr 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/tiktok-content-strategy-guide)

### Inferences
- Cut every frame before the claim. No logo, no "in this video," no slow fade. The first frame is the diagram, the number, the impossible image, or the sentence.
- Do not open a faceless video with a greeting. There is no person for the greeting to belong to, and that archetype is where faceless loses the most.
- Keep text to one idea at a time. Change it when the spoken line changes. A paragraph on screen is the untested version of a mistake the ads data already punishes at high density.
- Prefer a designed character or a diagram over a photoreal AI person if the goal includes likes and rewatches, not only curiosity views.
- Pacing after the hook: a new visual at each beat of the sentence. The pipeline can cut on the phrase. Three beats, then stop, beats one long take with no change.

### Gaps
- No first-second retention curve for logo stings, "hey guys," walls of text, static frames, or mismatched audio on organic TikTok in 2025–2026.
- The logo study TikTok ran in 2021 is cited by title only. The effect size is not public on the page retrieved.
- "Stock-looking AI face" as distinct from other AI video is not a category in the Revid labels.

## Are there documented faceless or motion-graphics accounts that grew fast in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
No independently audited case study of a faceless or motion-graphics account's 2025–2026 growth was found, with a name, a format, and a follower or view scale that a second source confirms. What exists are single videos inside a research corpus, a vendor's own account, and unsourced claims on X. Do not plan against a fake account.

No page name, no view count. The post itself has 536 likes, so the observation circulated. ### Inferences
- The closest real pattern to copy is not an account growth hack. It is a repeatable faceless sports or history voiceover that opens on one unfair or impossible fact, then shows the evidence. The Evan Hand videos are the sourced examples, and they are concentrated in one creator cluster, so they may not generalize.
- A motion-graphics account can take the Proof Drop lane (finance, science, "how this works") because that hook loses almost nothing without a face. There is no sourced account that proves the growth speed of that lane in 2025–2026.
- The mannequin loop is the format spec for generated clips: no face to keep consistent, one action, an on-screen count, a perfect loop, under 6 seconds. Test it. Do not cite it as a known viral account.
- Ignore unsourced "263 million views" and "millions of followers in 90 days" claims from tool blogs and X. They are the kind of number this file was told not to invent, and the primary posts do not establish them.

### Gaps
- No third-party audit of a named faceless or kinetic-type account's follower growth, post count, or view distribution for 2025–2026.
- TikTok Creative Center top-ads and creator spotlights were not pulled for a current motion-graphics account. The Showbiz names in circulation trace to 2022.
- Whether animation-led pages (as opposed to voiceover-plus-stock) are a growing class in 2026 is not broken out in viral.app, which says it has no reliable public AI label, or in Revid's account-level data, which is not public.
